# The Tare Shopfront Standard

A standard for the surface a business exposes to agents, so an agent can complete a named job without a human in the middle.

[The Agent Standard](/agent) asks what a bot does when a guest talks to it. This standard asks the mirror question: what a business makes available to an agent that never opens the page. The unit is **one property × one job family** — a hotel that takes a booking from an agent and cannot change it is not a worse hotel, it is a hotel at a lower level for that job.

This standard does not use the words "agent readiness". A phrase a platform product already means is not a measurement, and a measurement is what these pages print.

## The claim

The standard's claim is a **level, a grade and a job family**, printed together and never separately:

> Tare Shopfront Standard, **Level 3 · Grade B**, hotel booking, judged 2026-11-14.

- A **level** (1–5) is the last link in the chain that works end to end. It is not an average.
- A **grade** (A–F) says how well the links that do work behave.
- The **job family** says which job the level describes. A level without a job is not a claim this standard makes.

A bare "Level 4" is not a claim either, and neither is a bare grade. A Level 2 A is not comparable to a Level 4 A, and a Level 4 for booking says nothing about cancellations.

## Conformance

A property **shall** be able to state, for each job family it claims:

- its level, its grade, and the job family the pair describes;
- the date and the edition of this standard the pair was judged against;
- the receipt that shows the scan observed the surface rather than being told about it.

Anything else — a screenshot, a vendor's own page, a verbal account of the API — is a report about the surface, not a conformance claim under this standard. The levels, grades and jobs are the normative text; the explanation around them is informative.

## The four capabilities

| Capability | The question the scan asks |
|---|---|
| **Find** | can an agent read the facts it needs, in a form a program can use? |
| **Act** | can an agent start the job without a human opening the page? |
| **Confirm** | does the agent get a durable, machine-readable receipt? |
| **Recover** | can the agent change, cancel or refund the job end to end? |

## The two cross-cutting checks

Two checks run across all four and are never a fifth capability.

| Check | The question the scan asks |
|---|---|
| **Admit** | is an agent let in at all, and by which layer? |
| **Disclose** | what does the business say to an agent — that it is dealing with one, its terms, and what it does with the data? |

**Admit** asks whether an agent is let in at all — discovery, the property's site, the booking engine, the payment step — because a door that never opens makes the rest of the chain moot. **Disclose** asks what the business says to an agent: that it is dealing with one, its terms, and what it does with the data. The four capabilities are the chain; the level is where the chain stops, and a surface that refuses an agent has no level at all.

## Who this is for

The owner of a hotel, restaurant or clinic who has an agent answering at the front desk and needs an independent check before guests depend on it. The agency that built the agent is a partner in the reading, not the payer.

## What this standard is not

- **Not a certification, an accreditation or a compliance claim.** Nothing here says a property meets a law, a regulation or a payment-card standard.
- **Not a chatbot review.** A widget can be excellent and the surface open to no agent at all; the scan reads the surface.
- **Not a search-ranking or AI-visibility product.** Being found by a search engine is not the same as exposing a fact an agent can act on.
- **Not a single score.** There is no composite number, and a level is never summed with a grade.
- **Not a public leaderboard.** A property's level is its own reading, not a position in a ranking.
